## Why there's no cookie banner

Article 22.2 of [Spanish Law 34/2002 (LSSI-CE)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/doc.php?id=BOE-A-2002-13758), which brings [Directive 2002/58/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32002L0058) into Spanish law, generally requires consent before information is stored on or read from your device. It makes an exception when this is strictly necessary to provide a service you explicitly request.

Your theme preference falls under that exception: it is saved only when you choose a theme, and only to remember that choice. The [Spanish Data Protection Agency's (AEPD) guidance on cookies](https://www.aepd.es/guias/guia-cookies.pdf) also treats preferences selected by the user, such as the website's appearance, as exempt from consent when used only for that purpose.

The visit statistics described below don't store anything on your device either. So nothing on this website needs your consent before you start browsing.

## Visit statistics

[Vercel Web Analytics](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics) counts visits so I can see which pages are read and where visitors come from. It sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser. Instead, it groups the page views of the same visitor using a hash calculated from the request, which resets every 24 hours, so visits can't be linked across days or websites. According to [Vercel's documentation](https://vercel.com/docs/analytics/privacy-policy), it collects no identifiers that track people across websites, and I only see aggregated figures. The [privacy policy](/privacy) lists the data each page view records.

---

## How to block visit statistics

If you'd rather not be counted at all, a browser extension that blocks analytics scripts, such as [uBlock Origin](https://ublockorigin.com/), prevents the statistics request from being sent. The rest of the website works the same without it.
